Minor in Political Science

A minor in political science complements other fields of study by helping students understand law, political power, and the institutions that shape everyday life. Students develop analytical skills that complement studies in the humanities (including literature, history, performance, and philosophy), as well as scientific and professional fields. For example:

  • Public health and business students can examine how injustice and political systems affect their professional work.
  • Natural science and engineering students can explore the relationships of power they may encounter in their careers.

Requirements

To minor in Political Science, students must complete 18 total credit hours from POLS courses.

An introductory POLS course at the 100 or 200-level (3 credits):

  • This course serves as the prerequisite for the following 300-level courses

One of the following courses (3 credits):

  • POLS 305: Global/Comparative Politics (3)
  • POLS 315: Global Politics/International Relations (3)
  • POLS 335: History of Political Thought (3)
  • POLS 375: Constitutional Law: Institutions (3)
  • POLS 385: American Politics (3)

Any POLS courses at the 300-level or above, some examples may include (12 credits):

  • POLS 301: Hawaiʻi Politics (3)
  • POLS 302: Native Hawaiian Politics (3)
  • POLS 309: Indigenous Language Revitalization (3)
  • POLS 317: International Law (3)
  • POLS 339: Feminist Theory (3)
  • POLS 341: The Politics of Media (3)
  • POLS 342: Political Design & Futuristics (3)
  • POLS 374: Law, Politics and Society (3)
  • POLS 390: Political Inquiry & Analysis (3)
  • POLS 405: Executive Internship (up to 6 credits)
